Programmes

The right summer for every age

All programmes include 15 hours of English a week, full-board accommodation, a daily activity programme and weekly excursions. What changes is the style, pace and independence.

Ages 8–12

Junior Explorers

A gentle, joyful first taste of studying abroad. Lessons are built around themed weekly projects, like designing a treasure island or putting on a mini-musical, so learning English feels like an adventure.

What's it like?

  • Topic-based lessons full of games, songs, storytelling and crafts
  • Small classes of up to 12, grouped by level and age
  • A named house parent who checks in with each child every day
  • Structured free time and an earlier bedtime (21:00)
  • A weekly photo update and a short progress note for parents
  • Unaccompanied minor service and airport transfers available

Lesson focus

Speaking confidence, everyday vocabulary, listening and early reading and writing, all tied to what students experience on campus and on excursions.

Ages 13–15

Teen Discovery

Lively, communicative English for teenagers who want to speak with confidence. Students work in international teams on real projects, like making a podcast, running a campaign or filming a short documentary.

What's it like?

  • Interactive lessons focused on fluency, pronunciation and real-life English
  • Weekly team project presented on Friday to the whole school
  • A wide choice of afternoon clubs: sport, drama, music, art and STEM
  • Mixed-nationality rooms and activity groups to build friendships
  • Supervised shopping time on excursions
  • Optional extra English (+5 hours per week)

Lesson focus

Speaking and listening confidence, grammar in context, vocabulary for school and social life, and the soft skills that come with teamwork.

Ages 16–17

Young Leaders

For older students getting ready for university, exams or a global career. Expect seminar-style classes, debate, public speaking and a leadership project, plus more freedom to explore.

What's it like?

  • Academic English: essay skills, critical thinking and presentations
  • Debate club and a public-speaking showcase at the end of each fortnight
  • Leadership workshops and a university-campus visit
  • Later curfew and supervised free time in small groups on excursions
  • Optional IELTS / Cambridge exam-skills lessons
  • Personal learning report with next-step recommendations

Lesson focus

Fluency and accuracy for academic settings, structuring arguments, note-taking, and confident presentations in front of an audience.

How we teach

Learning that actually sticks

Our teaching follows the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), so progress is clear and comparable to school at home.

1. Placement

A short online test before arrival and a speaking check on day one place every student in the right class.

2. Active lessons

Students speak for most of every lesson through pair work, role-play, games and projects.

3. Use it outside

Each week's language is put to use in activities and on excursions, so it sticks.

4. Certificate

Every student leaves with a CEFR-referenced certificate and a written teacher report.
